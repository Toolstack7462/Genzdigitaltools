# Zee — The Gen Z Guide · Original Design Brief

**Zee** is an original animated support character created by and for **Gen Z Digital Store**. It is a
clean-room work: all artwork, SVG paths, state logic, schemas, animation timing and APIs are
designed from scratch for this project.

## Character
- **Name:** Zee — The Gen Z Guide.
- **Persona:** a friendly, professional digital guide — calm, helpful, never childish or noisy.
- **Silhouette:** an original rounded-geometric head (a soft "squircle" — a superellipse-style
  rounded square, deliberately *not* a plain circle) on simple shoulders. Reads as approachable tech,
  not a mascot cartoon.
- **Eyes:** an original expressive two-dot pupil system inside rounded-rectangle eye wells, with a
  single highlight dot and independent eyebrow bars for expression.
- **Mouth:** a minimal single-path mouth that morphs between a flat line, a gentle smile, a small
  "talking" oval, and a concerned curve.
- **Accessory:** a slim original **guide headset** (a thin headband arc + a small mic stub) — signals
  "support/guide" without copying any existing mascot.
- **Status badge:** a small corner dot/ring that reflects success/warning/error semantics (a second,
  non-motion communication channel for accessibility).

## Palette (existing Gen Z brand tokens)
- Navy `#071B33` (outlines / shoulders), Blue `#2563EB`, Cyan `#06B6D4`, Teal accent `#0E9F9A`.
- Success `#16A34A`, Warning `#D97706`, Error `#DC2626` (badge + concerned accents only).
- Light surfaces; high contrast; works on the white launcher and the panel header gradient.

## Distinctiveness
Zee is intentionally distinct from any external avatar product: squircle head (not a round ball),
rectangular eye wells (not round anime eyes), a guide **headset** accessory, and a brand navy/cyan/
teal palette. No external character artwork, screenshots, shapes, or schemas were referenced or
traced. See `THIRD_PARTY_DEPENDENCIES.md` for the explicit non-use statement.

## Sizing
Legible and balanced at the 48px launcher, 120–180px panel size, and on high-DPI displays (pure
vector; stroke widths scale with the viewBox, no raster assets).
